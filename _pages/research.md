<header class="page-intro"><div class="shell">
<p class="eyebrow">Research</p>
<h1>Making sense of seeing.</h1>
<p class="lead">How do visual features, movement, and neural representations work together to support object recognition? My research brings experimental and theoretical perspectives to questions about the organization of vision.</p>
</div></header>
<section class="section"><div class="shell"><div class="card-grid">
<article class="card"><span class="card-label">Visual cortex</span><h3>Functional organization</h3><p>The visual cortex contains strikingly systematic functional topographies. I’m interested in how these regularities emerge and what they tell us about visual processing.</p></article>
<article class="card"><span class="card-label">Object recognition</span><h3>Features and categories</h3><p>Curvature, contours, and statistical regularities contribute to object perception. My prior work explored curvilinear feature processing in humans and macaques.</p></article>
<article class="card"><span class="card-label">Motion</span><h3>Dynamic visual information</h3><p>Object motion supplies information beyond static appearance. I’ve studied how motion signals relate to recognition and category-selective responses.</p></article>
</div></div></section>
