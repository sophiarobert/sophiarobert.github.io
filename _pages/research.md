  <div class="shell">
    <div class="section-heading">
      <div><p class="eyebrow">Current research · New York University</p><h2>Individual differences in peripheral vision</h2></div>
    </div>
    <div class="prose">
      <p>Working with Jonathan Winawer, I am investigating relationships between retinotopic cortical organization and individual differences in peripheral visual performance. Of particular interest are cortical surface area and population receptive field properties in visual areas V1–V4, and their potential relationships to visual crowding and discrimination of natural-scene metamers.</p>
      <p>These projects combine psychophysical measurements with functional MRI and computational observer models. They examine the extent to which anatomical and functional properties of visual cortex account for variation in performance, and whether observer models of visual encoding predict perceptual discriminability across stimuli and individuals.</p>
      <p class="note">Ongoing research. These descriptions refer to research questions and approaches rather than established results.</p>

<section class="section section-contrast">
  <div class="shell">
    <div class="section-heading"><div><p class="eyebrow">Doctoral research · Carnegie Mellon University</p><h2>Cortical organization and plasticity following pediatric resection</h2></div></div>
    <div class="prose">
      <p>My doctoral research with Marlene Behrmann examined visual cortical organization in individuals who underwent cortical resection to treat drug-resistant epilepsy during childhood. This population provides an opportunity to investigate the extent to which the developing visual system maintains or reorganizes functional specialization following substantial changes to cortical anatomy.</p>
      <p>Using naturalistic fMRI and functional connectivity analyses, I characterized the organization of large-scale functional networks in the structurally intact hemisphere. This work identified deviations in functional connectivity and network organization relative to neurotypical comparison participants, alongside evidence of substantial preservation of visual function. Related collaborative projects investigated category selectivity, attention, and face and word recognition following pediatric resections.</p>

    <div class="section-heading"><div><p class="eyebrow">Prior research · National Institutes of Health</p><h2>Object representations derived from static and dynamic visual information</h2></div></div>
    <div class="prose">
      <p>At the National Institute of Mental Health, I studied neural representations of object categories and visual features in humans and macaques. This research addressed the degree to which object category information is represented through static image properties, dynamic motion cues, and curvilinear features.</p>
      <p>Using behavioral methods, fMRI, and single-unit electrophysiological recordings, this work investigated how distinct sources of visual information contribute to object-category responses across visual cortex. An associated motion-stimulus gallery provides examples of the stimuli used in this research area.</p>
